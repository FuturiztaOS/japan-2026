🕐Day Plan
Land
✈️ Arrive Narita — allow 90min for immigration + bags
Busiest arrival point in Japan. Queue moves fast but plan for it. Luggage first, then customs declaration, then immigration. Do NOT join any random queues — follow "Arrivals" signs only.
+2hr
🎫 Buy N'EX + Suica at JR machines
Green machines at B1F. Buy N'EX reserved seat first (¥3,070 to Shinjuku). Then buy Suica IC card — ¥500 deposit + load ¥3,000–5,000. This card = your metro pass + convenience store card for the whole trip.
+3.5hr
🏠 Arrive Shinjuku — check in Airbnb
Drop bags. First rule of Japan: take off shoes at entrance. Shower. Change. Absorb that you're in Tokyo.
📍 Shinjuku Station
19:00
🏪 7-Eleven / FamilyMart konbini run — MANDATORY
Japanese convenience stores are a different species. Buy: Onigiri ¥150 (tuna mayo is king), Karaage-kun fried chicken ¥230, Chu-hi fruit beer ¥220, Meiji Kinoko no Yama chocolate ¥210, Hot miso soup ¥130. Eat in the apartment. This IS the experience.
📍 7-Eleven Shinjuku
20:00
🍜 Dinner: Ichiran Ramen Shinjuku
Solo-booth ramen — each person gets their own curtained cubicle. Zero social pressure. Order: Tonkotsu base, Firm noodles, Extra chashu, Rich broth. ¥1,200/person (~RM36). The most uniquely Japanese dining experience on the trip.
📍 Ichiran Shinjuku
21:30
🏮 Kabukicho walk — Tokyo's entertainment district
Just walk. Don't go into any clubs. Experience: 10-storey buildings covered in neon, game centres everywhere, Robot Restaurant area, streams of people at midnight. Turn around at the big archway.
📍 Kabukicho
22:30
🍶 Golden Gai — 250 tiny bars in 6 alleys
Each bar holds 6–8 people max. No cover charge usually. Just push open any door that looks lit. ¥600–1,000/drink. Some bars have themes (horror, rock, film). It's chaotic, intimate, and unlike anywhere else on Earth. Stick together — alleys are narrow.
📍 Golden Gai
🌸 Sakura + Shibuya
Sunday Apr 5 · Shinjuku Gyoen → Harajuku → Shibuya Crossing
🌸 Shinjuku Gyoen opens at 9:00am sharp. Queue at gate by 8:45. Popular on weekends — gets crowded by 10:30. Early = peaceful cherry blossom moment.
🕐Day Plan

🌸 Shinjuku Gyoen — Cherry Blossom Hanami
¥500/person. Queue at Shinjuku gate at 8:45. Buy konbini breakfast beforehand. Best spots: French Formal Garden (wide lawns under rows of sakura), English Landscape Garden. Lay down on the grass. Take 1,000 photos. Allow 2hrs. Alcohol is banned in the park — save drinks for Golden Gai.

🏮 Asakusa + Akihabara
Monday Apr 6 · Ancient Tokyo → Future Tokyo
⏰ Senso-ji tip: Arrive before 9am. Magical before crowds arrive. By 10am it's packed with tour groups.
🕐Day Plan
08:30
🏮 Senso-ji Temple, Asakusa
Tokyo's oldest temple (628 AD). Free entry. Walk through Kaminarimon (Thunder Gate) — the lantern photo everyone has. Nakamise Shopping Street: buy Ningyo-yaki (red bean cakes) ¥500, Kaminari-okoshi (rice cracker) ¥800. Main hall: toss ¥5 coin (go-en = lucky connection), draw omikuji fortune. The orange ¥5 coin matters — not ¥1 or ¥10.
📍 Senso-ji
09:30
🗼 Asakusa Culture Tourist Info Center — 8F free view
Directly across from Kaminarimon. Free. Take lift to 8F observation floor — best aerial view of Senso-ji and the pagoda. Beats any paid observatory for THIS specific shot.
📍 Info Centre 8F
10:30

25min metro from Asakusa (Ginza line → Ueno). Late bloom may still have petals. Shinobazu Pond: rent rowboats ¥700/30min for 2-person boat — rent 3 boats for 6 people. Ameya-Yokocho market nearby for street snacks.

¥500 entry. Bus from station or 30min walk. 13.35m bronze Buddha sitting since 1252. Survived earthquakes and tsunamis — the hall around it was washed away in 1498, Buddha has sat outside since. 🙏Japan Etiquette — Don't Break These
🚫
NO tipping — ever
Tipping is considered rude in Japan. Do not leave coins on the table. Service charge is included. Just say "Gochisosama deshita" (thank you for the meal) when leaving.
🔇
Quiet on trains — no calls, no speakerphone, no eating
Everyone uses earphones and speaks in whispers. Eating on normal trains = frowned upon (Shinkansen is OK). No phone calls. This is universal and strictly observed.
👟
Remove shoes at temples, ryokans, some restaurants
Look for a step-up at the entrance. If shoes are lined up near the door = remove yours. Slip-on shoes recommended for this trip.
🗑️
No rubbish bins — carry your trash
Almost no public bins in Tokyo. Keep a bag in your daypack for rubbish. Dispose at konbini or your accommodation.
